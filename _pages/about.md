Hi, there! I am a second-year PhD candidate at <a href="https://github.com/tmlr-group">TMLR Group</a> in the <a href="https://eng.unimelb.edu.au/">Faculty of Engineering and Information Technology</a>, <a href="https://www.unimelb.edu.au/">the University of Melbourne</a>, advised by <a href="https://scholar.google.com.au/citations?user=eqe3JS8AAAAJ&hl=en&oi=ao">Dr. Feng Liu</a> and <a href="https://sites.google.com/view/liuhua-peng">Dr. Liuhua Peng</a>. I received my Master's degree from the <a href="https://ai.nju.edu.cn/">School of Artificial Intelligence</a> at <a href="https://www.nju.edu.cn/en/">Nanjing University</a> in 2024, where I was supervised by <a href="https://www.lamda.nju.edu.cn/gaow/">Dr. Wei Gao</a> and was a member of the <a href="https://www.lamda.nju.edu.cn/MainPage.ashx">LAMDA Group</a>, led by Professor <a href="https://cs.nju.edu.cn/zhouzh/">Zhihua Zhou</a>. Prior to that, I received my B.Eng. degree from <a href="https://en.dlut.edu.cn/">Dalian University of Technology</a> in 2021. 

My research interests lie in trustworthy AI, with a focus on developing statistically rigorous methods for reliable evaluation, safety assessment, and decision-making in modern AI systems.
<ul>
  <li>
    <strong>Certified LLM safety and evaluation.</strong>
    My current research particularly focuses on certified and cost-efficient evaluation of large language models, as well as the certified detection of training data contamination. More broadly, I am interested in developing theory-driven methods with finite-sample validity and provable error control for LLM evaluation and safety assessment, especially in adaptive and label-efficient settings.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>Data-adaptive, learnable testing beyond rigid hypothesis specification.</strong>
    Conventional hypothesis testing typically requires specifying formal hypotheses in advance and then designing a test statistic, which often forces machine learning problems into restrictive statistical formulations and limits practical adoption. My research aims to build learnable testing frameworks in which both the test statistic and the testing procedure are constructed from data, enabling rigorous evaluation of a broader class of questions.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>Single-example testing for probabilistic ML outputs.</strong>
    Most classical statistical tests are designed for batch samples, while many machine learning applications require decisions on individual examples with probabilistic outputs. This motivates my research on single-example testing methods that provide per-example statistical significance and theoretical false-alarm control.
  </li>
</ul>
