Hi, there! I am a second-year PhD candidate at <a href="https://github.com/tmlr-group">TMLR Group</a> in the <a href="https://eng.unimelb.edu.au/">Faculty of Engineering and Information Technology</a>, <a href="https://www.unimelb.edu.au/">the University of Melbourne</a>, advised by <a href="https://scholar.google.com.au/citations?user=eqe3JS8AAAAJ&hl=en&oi=ao">Dr. Feng Liu</a> and <a href="https://sites.google.com/view/liuhua-peng">Dr. Liuhua Peng</a>. I received my Master's degree from the <a href="https://ai.nju.edu.cn/">School of Artificial Intelligence</a> at <a href="https://www.nju.edu.cn/en/">Nanjing University</a> in 2024, where I was supervised by <a href="https://www.lamda.nju.edu.cn/gaow/">Dr. Wei Gao</a> and was a member of the <a href="https://www.lamda.nju.edu.cn/MainPage.ashx">LAMDA Group</a>, led by Professor <a href="https://cs.nju.edu.cn/zhouzh/">Zhihua Zhou</a>. Prior to that, I received my B.Eng. degree from <a href="https://en.dlut.edu.cn/">Dalian University of Technology</a> in 2021. 

My research interests lie at the intersection of <strong>statistics</strong> and <strong>trustworthy AI</strong>. At a general level, I am interested in two research directions: <strong>data-adaptive statistical hypothesis testing</strong>, which provides a foundation for theoretical guarantees, and <strong>statistically rigorous methods for evaluation and trustworthiness assessment</strong> in modern AI systems, which bring these ideas into practice. Specifically, my current research focuses on:
<ul>
  <li>
    LLM evaluation with fewer labeled samples while maintaining finite-sample validity and provable error control.
  </li>
  <li>
    End-to-end statistical hypothesis testing frameworks that derive both test statistics and testing procedures from data, while circumventing the need to fit complex AI tasks into restrictive classical hypothesis testing formulations.
  </li>
</ul>
As AI systems are increasingly used in real-world settings, building <strong>trustworthy AI</strong> is both important and challenging. Traditional machine learning theory provides important foundations, but many of its classical paradigms rely on <strong>simplified assumptions</strong> that do not fully match how modern AI systems are used in practice. This motivates my interest in data-driven statistical methods, which <strong>rely directly on observed data</strong> rather than simplified assumptions about how AI systems behave, and therefore can provide <strong>more realistic and reliable guarantees</strong> for evaluating and trusting AI systems.
